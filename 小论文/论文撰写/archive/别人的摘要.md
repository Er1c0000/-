Abstract—Accurate jitter analysis is critical for nextgeneration I/O designs. In this paper, a method to separate deterministic jitter (DJ) and random jitter (RJ) is proposed, which first obtains the Time Interval Error (TIE) histogram of the signal, then uses the Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) algorithm to smooth the histogram, and then defines a fitting parameter to perform a Dual-Dirac fit on the smoothed histogram to estimate DJ and RJ. Finally, the estimation results of the method are given, and the estimation errors of deterministic jitter and random jitter are 1.2% and 4.7%, respectively, which verifies the effectiveness of the method.

Abstract—In a high-speed digital communication system, jitter performance plays a crucial role in bit-error rate. It is impor tant to accurately derive each type of jitter as well as total jitter and to identify root causes of jitter by jitter decomposition as a keytool.Inthispaper,weextendourpreviouslyreportedalgorithm to decompose duty cycle distortion as well as other deterministic jitter components including periodic jitter (PJ), intersymbol inter ference (ISI), and random jitter (RJ), and describe more detail of this algorithm with extensively simulation and measurements to verify the algorithm. The proposed ISI model only requires the time invariant condition, which is true for almost all systems, while the traditional ISI model is valid only with linear time invariant assumption. Compared to the conventional methods, the proposed jitter decomposition method is able to obtain estimated individ ual jitter component values with good accuracy by using fewer samples of total jitter data. The efficiency and accuracy of the proposed method were demonstrated by simulation and hardware experiments.

 Abstract With the increasing demand for timing accuracy in electronic systems, jitter control has emerged as a critical challenge in high-speed digital communications. To effectively suppress jitter, decomposition methods based on equivalent-time sampling oscilloscopes are employed to identify its sources. However, jitter decomposition often requires extensive sampling points, leading to prolonged measurement times that fail to meet the stringent real-time requirements of online testing and adaptive clock recovery. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a two-stage jitter decomposition algorithm based on dual-Dirac model, which decomposes deterministic jitter and random jitter. The proposed method employs a two-stage decomposition process: (1) an initial coarse estimation using maximum a posteriori-gradient descent, followed by (2) Markov chain Monte Carlo-based parameter refinement. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance, with Kulback-Leibler divergence reduced to 0.056 at 64 sampling points, providing a 17.21% improvement in accuracy compared to Gaussian mixture model and a 63.19% enhancement over kernel density estimation, while the relative bias of parameter significance outperforms baseline schemes across different numbers of samples.
